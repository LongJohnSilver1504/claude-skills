# Conversion copy

Voice, tone, capitalization and microcopy rules belong to the interface-copy skill; this file holds the landing-page-specific patterns.

## Layout selection (say why)

| Type | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| A. Classic hero plus sections | the product is understandable from a hero capture — most common |
| B. Long-form story | you must educate and overcome skepticism |
| C. Minimal conversion page | high-intent traffic (email to known users) or a short offer (download, waitlist) |
| D. Comparison page | search intent includes alternatives ("X vs Y", "best for") |

## Above the fold (required)

1. Headline — outcome plus audience.
2. Subheadline — clarifies how, adds specificity.
3. Primary CTA — verb plus what they get.
4. One proof signal — logo strip, a real stat, a short testimonial.
5. Hero visual — product capture or video, or a strong illustration.

## Headline formulas

- "{Outcome} without {pain}"
- "The {category} for {audience}"
- "Ship {result} in {time}"
- "{Do the thing} in {small number} {unit}"

Sentence case. No hyphenated compounds the copy can avoid. Two lines maximum in the hero at 1280px; break where the thought breaks.

## Conversion rules

- **Match message to source.** Ad traffic sees the ad's promise and visual tone mirrored in the hero.
- **One next step.** One primary CTA; never two competing above the fold; the final CTA is identical to the first.
- **Benefit first.** Features are what it does; benefits are what that means for them. **Bold benefit** — then the detail: *Faster iteration — generate three layout variants in one click.*
- **Be specific.** "Cut your weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes" over "Save time and streamline".
- **Reduce risk.** At least one of: free trial, free plan, no credit card, cancel anytime, money-back guarantee.
- **Objections are a section.** FAQ of 6–12 questions, plain Q&A (also feeds answer engines); earlier on the page for high-friction offers. Proof sits next to the claim it supports, never only at the bottom.

## Section copy

- Section headings state the benefit of the section, not its category ("Know where every truck is" over "Features").
- One tagline moment mid-page is allowed as its own large-type beat (a benefit statement of two lines minimum) — one, not on every section.
- Captions never narrate what the visual already shows; if the visual labels itself, cut the caption.

## Content realism

No lorem ipsum, "John Doe", "Acme Corp"; organic numbers (`47.2%`, `$99.00`), varied dates, unique avatars; no AI clichés (Elevate, Seamless, Unleash, Next-gen, Game changer, Delve, Tapestry, "In the world of"); active voice; no exclamation marks in success copy; no "Oops". **No invented metrics, logos, testimonials or counts** — an honest `—` "metric to confirm" placeholder or a section that needs no proof slot.

## SEO / AEO

Index evergreen offers whose promise matches search intent: a clear `<title>`, meta description, internal links from the homepage and feature pages, FAQ in plain question-and-answer form (FAQ schema where appropriate). `noindex` ad-only campaign pages and time-bound offers.

## Output before building

1. Page outline — sections in order, each tagged with its arc beat.
2. Hero copy — headline, subheadline, CTA, proof line.
3. Benefits — three to five outcome-driven bullets.
4. How it works — three steps.
5. FAQ — six to twelve questions and answers.
6. Index or noindex, plus title and meta if indexed.
7. Shape and layout type, and why; the rotation statement.

Then build section by section.
