# Narrative arc

Three questions govern a landing page. `page-shapes.md` answers *what shape is the whole page*. The archetypes answer *what does each section look like*. This file answers *why does each section exist, and in what order* — the question whose absence produces pages where every section is well-built and the page adds up to nothing.

Grounded in StoryBrand (customer is the hero, brand is the guide: character, problem, guide, plan, call to action, failure, success), PAS (problem–agitate–solution), and the converged SaaS convention (hero → problem → solution → how it works → proof → CTA).

## The default arc

| Beat | Job | Typical treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Hook | the promise, sized and scoped by the hero rules | any hero archetype |
| 2. Problem / stakes | what is broken or at risk, specific to this product; what happens if nothing changes | short prose + a supporting visual of the broken state, a before/after, or folded into the hero lede on a short arc — the sharpest beat on the page, never a wall of text |
| 3. Solution / mechanism | how the product fixes it, shown | alternating bands, bento tiles, an annotated capture |
| 4. How it works | 3–5 concrete steps | numbered sequence, spec sheet |
| 5. Proof | real evidence beside the claim it supports | logo wall, pull quote, stat strip — every number real |
| 6. Close | the ask, echoing the hook's promise | statement + action, inline form, sticky bar |

## Minimum count

Hook + problem + solution + proof + close is five. **Never below four distinct beats.** Hook → proof → close skips the part that persuades. "How it works" may fold into "solution" for a simple product — a deliberate merge, said out loud.

A Manifesto shape may spend most of its length on problem/stakes with a one-line solution; a Stat-Led page may fold proof into the hero. Fine, when stated and tied to the shape and the argument.

## Proof has a floor

A label + a sentence + a small icon in a bordered box is not a proof beat. Proof needs real supporting visuals — an annotated capture, a real chart, a constructed diagram, a real quote with a real name. Undersized cards floating in empty background fail the beat even when they pass the layout rules.

## Self-check before finalizing

Reading top to bottom, the page answers in order:

1. Who is this for, and what's the promise?
2. What's actually wrong, or at stake?
3. How does this fix it?
4. Why should I believe it?
5. What do I do now?

A section that serves none of these — or a stated variant — is a gap in the arc, not a style choice. This is a different check from structural variety: a page can rotate through every shape and still read as sections with no throughline.

## Recording

State the beat sequence out loud alongside the shape pick: *"Arc: hook (H2 split demo) → problem (prose + before/after) → solution (F1 bands) → how (F4 steps) → proof (P4 stat strip) → close (C2 statement). No beats skipped."* Put it in the CSS stamp and the `.design/log.json` entry so a later review can check the arc held together.
